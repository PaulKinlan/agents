## Evidence and choice

During active landing on 2026-10-13, a read-only 30-second `/proc/locks` sample at 100ms resolution saw 299 samples, 99.7% held, mean 3.46 waiters (max 5), and 9 completed holder turnovers. Median observed turnover was about 2.12s, max 13.85s; first/last holds were censored, and the turnover interval can include a release-to-reacquire gap, so these are approximate, not exact per-call holds. The observed new waiter/holder PIDs by lane totaled at least 15 in 30s (fast calls between samples were missed). Four earlier calls each failed a 90s wait despite continuous service. Increasing the timeout cannot fix a waiter that polls after other callers have reacquired the lock.

The uncontended `flock -n` fast path is unchanged. Under contention, the shim now queues **blocking** `flock` on the existing lock fd *before* first-holder diagnostics; `timeout -k 2 "$wait" flock "$fd"` bounds the blocking child, while a reporter that closes its inherited lock fd preserves elu6's first/periodic holder messages. The parent and blocking child share the open-file description, so a successful lock remains held by the parent and `bd` payload after the `flock` child exits. The default `$wait` is elu6's existing `FLEET_BD_LOCK_WAIT` of **300s**, inherited from the pre-elu6 shim's `flock -w 300` rather than guessed from these short holds. A bound expiry (including a kill after two more seconds) reports TIMEOUT and returns 75 **without executing `bd`**; the heartbeat is separate and cannot replace that bound. Zero and malformed bounds cannot turn `timeout 0` into an unbounded wait. The existing nested `FLEET_BD_LOCKED` path remains unchanged.

This joins the Linux kernel's lock wait queue instead of the one-second `sleep`/`flock -n` polling loop, avoiding the measured missed-release polling failure. **It does not promise strict FIFO priority** across all kernels or a maximum queue wait under overload. Batching related bead calls under a single short hold still reduces demand but cannot itself guarantee access. A hard FIFO requirement would need a separate ticket protocol with stale-ticket recovery, not an undocumented claim about this patch.

## Why the 300-second default remains (agents-atom)

An independent read-only `/proc/locks` instrument sampled the live VM for **59.8s at 445 samples** during active work: lock duty held **84.9%**, about **23 acquisitions/minute**, and observed individual holder durations median **1.9s**, mean **2.2s**, max **13.5s**. This is a holder distribution, **not** the distribution of a caller's successful time waiting in the queue. The earlier 30s/299-sample result above measures holder *turnovers* at 100ms resolution and cannot supply a successful-wait p99 either. Both windows are too short to establish the tail of a network `bd dolt push`.

A maximum holder duration of X does **not** bound a queued wait to X. Acquisition can wait through the remaining current hold **plus the sum of holds ahead of it**; for example, three 13.5s holders ahead imply about 40.5s before acquisition, even though each holder is below 20s. End-to-end `bd` call time then adds the caller's own operation **after** acquisition. Thus changing the default to 20s from the measured one-holder maximum would turn a healthy crowded queue into a false refusal. Batching calls lowers demand but does not change this arithmetic.

**Decision:** retain the pre-existing **300s** `FLEET_BD_LOCK_WAIT` default for compatibility while the new blocking queue removes the missed-release failure. It is a **safety net, not a performance knob**, and 300s is not claimed to be a measured optimum or a derived p99. The independent holder heartbeat makes a long wait visible; if the bound fires, the shim reports TIMEOUT and exits 75 without executing `bd`. Before reducing the default, measure a *longer busy-window distribution of successful acquisition waits*, queue depth at arrival, and successful network `bd dolt push` hold tails; choose a bound with headroom for the queue depth and tails it must tolerate. Keep `FLEET_BD_LOCK_WAIT` available for an operator who explicitly needs a different bound. This documentation is a follow-up to cleared nbka @03fae05; its runtime patch is unchanged.

Five additional runs passed. It also asserts that zero/malformed bounds return 75 without calling bd.
- **Heavy saturation:** one raw holder for 5s and twelve queued shim callers, each holding the lock for a 0.15s stub operation. The test waits until each caller appears in `/proc/locks` before admitting the next, records monotonic arrival/entry timestamps and asserts measured entry order 1..12 and zero timeouts. The barrier matches **major:minor:inode**, never inode alone; an injected same-inode/different-device waiter must NOT count, while an exact-key waiter must. Final patch-application run with an inherited reviewer role and nested marker (which the fixture clears): **12/12, order 1..12, no starvation at the 12s bound; wait seconds min 5.004, lower median 5.673, nearest-rank p90 6.348, max 6.488**. Earlier heavy runs also passed in order without timeouts; their observed maximum waits ranged from 5.900s to 6.729s (including the pre-artifact full-device-key run at 6.435s). This is an observation on this Linux kernel under saturation, not a universal FIFO guarantee.
