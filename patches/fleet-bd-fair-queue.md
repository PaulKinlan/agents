## Evidence and choice

During active landing on 2026-10-13, a read-only 30-second `/proc/locks` sample at 100ms resolution saw 299 samples, 99.7% held, mean 3.46 waiters (max 5), and 9 completed holder turnovers. Median observed turnover was about 2.12s, max 13.85s; first/last holds were censored, and the turnover interval can include a release-to-reacquire gap, so these are approximate, not exact per-call holds. The observed new waiter/holder PIDs by lane totaled at least 15 in 30s (fast calls between samples were missed). Four earlier calls each failed a 90s wait despite continuous service. Increasing the timeout cannot fix a waiter that polls after other callers have reacquired the lock.

The uncontended `flock -n` fast path is unchanged. Under contention, the shim now queues **blocking** `flock` on the existing lock fd *before* first-holder diagnostics; `timeout -k 2 "$wait" flock "$fd"` bounds the blocking child, while a reporter that closes its inherited lock fd preserves elu6's first/periodic holder messages. The parent and blocking child share the open-file description, so a successful lock remains held by the parent and `bd` payload after the `flock` child exits. The default `$wait` is elu6's existing `FLEET_BD_LOCK_WAIT` of **300s**, inherited from the pre-elu6 shim's `flock -w 300` rather than guessed from these short holds. A bound expiry (including a kill after two more seconds) reports TIMEOUT and returns 75 **without executing `bd`**; the heartbeat is separate and cannot replace that bound. Zero and malformed bounds cannot turn `timeout 0` into an unbounded wait. The existing nested `FLEET_BD_LOCKED` path remains unchanged.

This joins the Linux kernel's lock wait queue instead of the one-second `sleep`/`flock -n` polling loop, avoiding the measured missed-release polling failure. **It does not promise strict FIFO priority** across all kernels or a maximum queue wait under overload. Batching related bead calls under a single short hold still reduces demand but cannot itself guarantee access. A hard FIFO requirement would need a separate ticket protocol with stale-ticket recovery, not an undocumented claim about this patch.
